Fig. 5: focus, logic and editorial choices

The one message

The phantom cage is exactly harmonic, with no free parameter, up to Δ* ≈ 2.6σi (U* ≈ 3.5 kBT) in every network we measured. Beyond that point junctions move together with their neighbourhood. This cooperative mode starts at the same place in every condition and is insensitive to swelling. Its weight is set by strand length and by temperature. Everything in the figure either establishes this claim or protects it against the obvious objection, that the tails come from heterogeneity or from poor statistics.

Data-consistency flags (read before finalising)

  1. σi distribution vs pooled width. For PEGDA 5 kDa (2 h), the σi plot gives a mean of 12.5 nm (s.d. 5.6 nm, range 1.5–25 nm), but the Gaussian fit to the pooled P(δx) of the same sample gives 9.4 nm. A pooled variance cannot be smaller than ⟨σi²⟩ = (13.7 nm)². A mixture of Gaussians with the plotted σi would also have heavier pooled tails than measured. Possible causes: σi is a 2D or radial quantity, it includes localisation error, or the two plots come from different gels or pipelines. P̂ depends on σi, so resolve this first.
  2. Same sample, two answers. PEGDA 20 kDa (2 h) appears in the mass series (λ = 1.24, E3σ = 10.3) and in the swelling series (λ = 0.77, E3σ = 5.9). The spread between gels is therefore about ×1.7, and the "λ rises with mass" claim rests on the 5 kDa vs 10–20 kDa difference. Add replicate gels (n ≥ 3) before stating a mass trend beyond 10 kDa.
  3. Numbers from the previous draft are not reproduced. λ = 0.65 → 1.10 and F = 0.50 / 0.23 pN do not match any plot in the deck. The Origin exponential lines give λ = 0.60 / 0.69 / 0.90 (mass) and 0.80 → 0.55 (5 → 45 °C). Log-space, count-weighted fits over |Δ| ≥ 2.5 give 0.63 / 0.80 / 1.24 and 0.84 / 0.79 / 0.69 / 0.85 / 0.54. The difference comes from the fit window and weighting; a linear-scale least-squares fit overweights the crossover region. Use maximum likelihood on the raw |Δ| > Δ* values, and quote the window. Your own force plots give plateaus of 0.75, 0.54 and 0.46 pN (5, 10, 20 kDa). These agree with F ≈ 1.5 kBT/σ using the pooled σ. Use 0.5–0.75 pN.
  4. Δ* is not strictly independent of temperature. It rises from 2.52 to 2.85 (5 → 45 °C) and U* from 3.2 to 4.1 kBT. Each point is within about 1.5 s.e., but the trend is monotonic. Tail weight falls with temperature (E3σ from 8.7 to 3.3). Write "nearly constant" and report the trend. The statement "Δ* is unchanged by temperature, so the cost is entropic" needs revising (Section 5 draft, paragraph 7).
  5. The temperature series was swollen for 1 h and the others for 2 h. Say so in the legend (done).
  6. Placeholders: the split-half values in c are simulated from the measured σi; replace them with the real split-half σi. The "this study" band in i is an estimate from the trajectory plots (about 180 frames) and the MSD (0.06–10 s); replace it with the actual distribution of N. Then run the same Shapiro–Wilk test on the real trajectories and plot the measured fraction on the panel. The simulated curves use the real fitted P̂.
  7. All numbers were digitised from the Origin EMF files in the deck (data/), so they carry about 1% digitisation error. Re-run make_fig5.py on the raw histograms before submission.
